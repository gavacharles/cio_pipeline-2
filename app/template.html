04 · Audit trail

Every number can be traced back to a release

The panel ships with its own evidence: how vintages were chained, how it reconciles with official aggregates, and the time-series properties a reviewer will ask about first.

Reconciliation with official aggregates

Overlap window, monthly

Correlation of the CIPI headline with the consumer price index and the manufacturing producer price index over the same months. High agreement confirms the spliced series tracks the official picture; the residual index-point gap is the construction-specific signal.

Stationarity verdicts

ADF and KPSS, level and first difference

Most usable series are integrated of order one — difference-stationary — which is what the SVAR and forecasting layers assume. Level-stationary and inconclusive cases are the short or sparse series.

Structural breaks by series

Bai–Perron-style scan, BIC-selected

Each dot is a detected break in a series' level. The 2020 and 2022 columns are the pandemic and the commodity shock; the explorer above overlays the same dates.

Vintage splicing

Chain-linking across UBOS rebasings

A factor of 1.000 means consecutive releases agreed on the overlap; factors below one record a rebasing. Links with no overlapping months are flagged for manual review rather than fabricated.

05 · Research outputs

Five estimation models already run end to end

Each paper in the portfolio has a starter model wired to the panel. These are its current results, read straight from the repository outputs.

P4 · Which inputs move the market — the SIMI ranking

Diebold–Yilmaz spillovers from a VAR on monthly log-returns

Net spillover: the share of other materials' forecast-error variance a material explains, minus the share explained by others. Cement dominates the system; sand, timber and paint are mostly receivers.

Spillover table: row = receiving material, column = source. The diagonal is own-variance; brighter off-diagonal cells are stronger transmission.

P3 · Volatility clusters

Dynamic-time-warping k-means on log-returns

Materials whose price paths share a shape sit in the same cluster — the basis for procurement risk grouping and programme float.

P8 · The Dynamic Adjustment Mechanism, back-tested

Flagship product · stylised road, building and water projects

Under a fixed-price contract the contractor's margin erodes one-for-one as input costs drift. The DAM is a price-adjustment clause: when a Weighted Macro-Volatility Index — the basket's rolling material volatility plus the exchange-rate volatility scaled by the P6 sensitivity — breaches its trigger band, the contract sum is revised upward, capped per period and cumulative for the remainder of the contract.

The back-test picks the most volatile 18-month window in the panel (the 2022 commodity shock) and compares the final margin and its variance under a fixed price against the DAM, for three reference baskets built from CIPI weights. These are simulations on transparently stylised projects, not real contract data.

P9 · Does the network help forecast the CIPI?

Strict walk-forward evaluation, ridge regression on lags

The network-augmented model adds cross-material dispersion and pairwise-correlation features to the own-lag baseline. A Diebold–Mariano statistic above about 1.96 means the accuracy gain is unlikely to be noise.
